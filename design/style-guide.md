# Style guide (Pixel Pat)

Rules every texture and model in this project follows. Change a rule only when a job proves it wrong, and note why in `lessons.md`.

## Light
- One light source: **top-left, slightly in front** (screen direction x = -1, y = -1, z = +1.2, normalised).
- Lit side: top-left. Core shadow and outline darkening: bottom-right. Never shade evenly around the outline (pillow shading).
- Raised details (chunks, buttons, rivets) get a highlight edge on their top-left and a 1 px contact shadow on the surface below/right of them.

## Colour
- Limited palette per asset: about 8–12 colours, listed as hex in the asset's spec.
- Ramps are **hue-shifted**: highlights move towards yellow and lose some saturation, shadows move towards red/purple and lose a bit of saturation. Never make a shadow by only lowering brightness.
- The darkest colour of a ramp is never pure black; outline colours are a deep, warm hue of the object itself.

## Pixels
- Clusters, not noise: no orphan pixels (a pixel whose four neighbours all have another colour) unless placed on purpose (a single specular glint).
- No dithering on item textures at 16–64 px; use clean colour bands with irregular, lumpy borders instead.
- Silhouette first: the outline must read at 1× (16 px on screen). Check it before adding detail.
- Outline: selective. Darkest ramp colour on the shadow side, one step lighter on the lit side. Transparent background.

## Texel density and size
- Standard items: 16×16. Hero items seen large in the world (ItemDisplay, e.g. the big cookie): 64×64.
- Power-of-two sizes only.

## Process
- Every texture is generated by a script in `design/scripts/<asset>.py` (deterministic: fixed seeds) and written to its final location.
- Shared helpers live in `design/scripts/lib/` (`preview.py`: ×N nearest-neighbour previews and size strips).
- Review every PNG at ×8 nearest-neighbour, plus a 1×/×4 side-by-side, before committing.

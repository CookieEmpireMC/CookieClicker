# Style guide (Pixel Pat)

Rules every texture and model in this project follows. Change a rule only when a job proves it wrong, and note why in `lessons.md`.

## Light
- One light source: **top-left, slightly in front** (screen direction x = -1, y = -1, z = +1.2, normalised).
- Lit side: top-left. Core shadow and outline darkening: bottom-right. Never shade evenly around the outline (pillow shading).
- Raised details (chunks, buttons, rivets) get a highlight edge on their top-left and a 1 px contact shadow on the surface below/right of them.

## Colour
- Limited palette per asset: about 8–12 colours, listed as hex in the asset's spec.
- Ramps are **hue-shifted**: highlights move towards yellow and lose some saturation, shadows move towards red/purple and lose a bit of saturation. Never make a shadow by only lowering brightness.
- The darkest colour of a ramp is never pure black; outline colours are a deep, warm hue of the object itself.

## Pixels
- Clusters, not noise: no orphan pixels (a pixel whose four neighbours all have another colour) unless placed on purpose (a single specular glint).
- No dithering on item textures at 16–64 px; use clean colour bands with irregular, lumpy borders instead.
- Silhouette first: the outline must read at 1× (16 px on screen). Check it before adding detail.
- Outline: selective. Darkest ramp colour on the shadow side, one step lighter on the lit side. Transparent background.

## Texel density and size
- Standard items: 16×16. Small icons with a face or several parts (panel icons): 32×32. Hero items seen large in the world (ItemDisplay, e.g. the big cookie): 64×64.
- Font glyphs: 16×16 drawn for `height` 8 (clean 2× downscale), 1 px transparent margin top and bottom.
- 3D models: keep the texel density the same on every face (the cookie: 4 px per model unit on faces and edges).
- Power-of-two sizes only.

## Process
- Every texture is generated by a script in `design/scripts/<asset>.py` (deterministic: fixed seeds) and written to its final location.
- Shared helpers live in `design/scripts/lib/`: `preview.py` (×N nearest-neighbour previews and size strips), `pixel.py` (masks, height-field shading, outline, orphan cleanup), `model_render.py` (orthographic renderer for cuboid JSON models).
- Review every 3D model with `model_render.py` from at least front, 3/4, side and back-below before committing.

## 3D models (vanilla JSON)
- Round objects are stacks of non-overlapping slabs fitted to the texture's silhouette (no coplanar overlaps, so no z-fighting). Keep it under ~10 elements.
- When cuboids trim a painted outline, redraw the outline on the new silhouette in a derived face texture instead of leaving the cut edge.
- Edge/side textures carry no directional light (the object turns); the game's face shading does that.
- Objects that spin must look right from both sides: map the art un-mirrored on both faces.
- Review every PNG at ×8 nearest-neighbour, plus a 1×/×4 side-by-side, before committing.

## Block structures (house designs)
Mostly **medieval**: English/Tudor half-timber on a stone plinth, Norman stone ground floors, brick only for stacks and
ovens. Sources and reasoning: `docs/research/build-quality.md`. The lint (`design/build-lint.md`, `lib/build_lint.py`)
enforces the rules marked **[lint]**; the rest are judged in the textured previews and in the Chunky shots.

**How to build**
- Generated by `design/scripts/<design>.py` into `src/main/resources/houses/<design>.yml` from the kit library
  (`lib/build.py`: `Build`, `Ramp`, `Wall`, `window`, `door`, `gable_roof`, `stack`; `lib/scenes.py`; `lib/blocks.py`).
  Every block state is written in full; connections (panes, bars, fences, walls) and **stair shapes** are computed
  from the neighbours exactly like the game does on placement (`blocks.resolve`), and `house_lib.validate` re-checks them.
- Nothing may need support from outside the design; no gravity blocks (gravel only in the ground layers).
- Previews are textured with the real 26.3 client jar (`lib/tex_render.py`, `BAKERY_CLIENT_JAR`, default
  `~/bakery-review/chunky/home/resources/minecraft.jar`, read in place, never committed). Review front, 3/4 both sides,
  back and above, then the Chunky shots when the orchestrator renders them. At most 3 rounds per build.

**Ranked rules (R1-R21)**
1. **R1 Two volumes minimum** [lint]; a non-rectangular plan (hall + wing, annex, lean-to).
2. **R2 Roof heights differ**: one tallest element, asymmetry; the roof never covers more than about half of a facade
   (raise the walls before steepening the roof) [lint: two different roof heights].
3. **R3 Three depth planes per facade**: pilasters/jetty/brackets, the wall plane, recessed or proud details (sills,
   boxes, shutters, braces). **A flat run of more than 4 columns fails** [lint].
4. **R4 Timber frame**: primary posts (logs) at corners and bay ends, secondary studs or braces (planks), panels at most
   3 x 3; plaster is the lightest value, timber the darkest wall value, never a wall that is mostly timber.
5. **R5 Windows**: a sill (top-half trapdoor or slab), shutters on both sides or a flower box, and a lit interior
   (shelf + lantern a block behind the pane) [lint, every glass pane cluster].
6. **R6 Every material is a ramp of 3 or more blocks**, ordered by value: stone (stone_bricks, cracked, mossy, andesite),
   plinth (cobblestone, mossy cobblestone, andesite), plaster (white terracotta, calcite, light grey terracotta), brick
   (bricks, red nether bricks, mud bricks), timber (dark oak log, stripped log, planks), roof (the field stairs, the
   eave/rake stairs, a weathering patch), floor (spruce, oak, dark oak planks) [lint].
7. **R7 Variants come in clusters** of 2-6 blocks (noise, not rows), 10-30 % each, a dominant base of at least a third;
   weather where water runs (moss low, light grey under jetties) [lint: no even splat, no dominant block above 93 %].
8. **R8 Roofs**: eave overhang of at least 1 in a second material (spruce/dark oak), rake rows trimmed, ridge beam +
   slab cap, 45 degrees (or 22/26 for lean-tos), no flat glass cubes: dormer-style windows, gable windows, flush louvres
   [lint: eave and field differ, overhang >= 1].
9. **R9 Jetty**: on market/shop buildings the upper floor projects 1 block with inverted-stair brackets under the
   bressumer beam.
10. **R10 One shared family** for all builds: dark oak frame colour, stone-brick or cobble plinth, one lantern type,
    1-block eaves, flower boxes in terracotta pots.
11. **R11 At most ONE chimney per building** [lint, hard rule]. More fireplaces share that stack; flush ridge louvres,
    vents and turrets are not chimneys. A 2 x 2 brick shaft on a stone base, a corbel ring of inverted stairs, a
    two-slab crown, the top at least 2 above the nearest roof. `points.smoke` / `points.steam` have at most one entry.
12. **R12 Plinth of 1-2 courses** in the plinth ramp, and the wall above is a different material [lint].
13. **R13 Ground story**: a path to the door, a stoop, a bed or gravel apron around the walls (ground layers).
14. **R14 Ground ramps per place**: lanes gravel + coarse dirt, plazas cobblestone + mossy + andesite, yards coarse dirt
    + podzol, beds podzol + rooted dirt. Never three tones in one patch.
15. **R15 Nothing organic is straight for more than 7 blocks**: paths, aprons, beds and hedges wobble by one block every
    3-5 cells [lint on the ground layers and leaf rows].
16. **R16 No grid**: fence posts at irregular gaps (`post-spacing`), lanterns at irregular post counts, lamp posts only at
    doors and junctions [lint: at least 3 different gaps].
17. **R17 Furnished interiors** behind windows: wall shelf + lantern, oven glow, barrels.
18. **R18 Decor is scenes** (D1-D7), never loose blocks.
19. **R19 Front yard**: 5 deep x 7 wide clear in front of a door, a 3-wide corridor free at walking height [lint].
20. **R20 Trees**: custom shapes (trunk, 3-4 noisy canopy blobs, podzol roots, optional mound), leaves persistent.
21. **R21 Review**: textured render plus Chunky shots by the orchestrator; `Status: final` only with the user's approval.

**Decor rules (D1-D7)**
- **D1** every group has an anchor: a wall, door, window, fence, path, bed, tree, well or water; no lone prop [lint].
- **D2** a scene has at least 5 blocks of 3 types, or a ground patch of 9 cells [lint].
- **D3** the ground under and around a group changes in an irregular ring (podzol, coarse dirt, gravel).
- **D4** heights vary, odd counts (woodpile pyramid 3-2-1, crates 2+1).
- **D5** density: at most one scene per 6 blocks of facade; none in the front zone, the factory margin, the slot
  footprints or the hologram corridor (y >= 0); ground treatments may go everywhere except the house design box and the belt.
- **D6** lamp posts only at junctions, doors and the market, each on its own gravel apron.
- **D7** the small villager workers stay (item displays on the building slots); their yards get ground, not props.

**Palette family (verified ids)**: plaster `white_terracotta`/`calcite`/`light_gray_terracotta`; timber `dark_oak_log`,
`stripped_dark_oak_log`, `dark_oak_planks`; stone `stone_bricks`/`cracked`/`mossy`/`andesite`, plinth `cobblestone` ramp;
roof `spruce_stairs` or `mud_brick_stairs` field with `dark_oak_stairs` eave/rake; brick `bricks`/`red_nether_bricks`/
`mud_bricks`; waxed copper accents (never unwaxed); light: `lantern`, `waxed_copper_lantern`, lit `campfire`, `shroomlight`,
`magma_block`. `iron_chain` (the old `chain` is gone in 26.x).
- Factory/workshop buildings keep waxed copper for awnings/lintels; warm light sources for anything that should glow.
- Plot layouts (`layout:` in the design file) are validated in the generator: inside the fence ring, free cells, margins.

## Animated textures
- Scrolling surfaces (belts): one period of the pattern = frame count × 1 texel, built once and rolled per frame; `frametime` 2, `interpolate` false. Write the direction rule (which texture edge faces which world direction) in the spec.

## Cameras and viewpoints
- Every fixed camera point gets a perspective preview (`lib/voxel_render.render_perspective`) and a generator assert that the plot's extreme points stay inside 95% of a **4:3** frame at FOV 70.
- Prefer a raised 3/4 view over a straight-on one: it shows depth and keeps the foreground (gate) from hiding the pedestal.
